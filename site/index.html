How it works

Three things happen. That's all.

No complicated menus. You set the rules once, PaCo does the rest.

1

You set the rules

On the PaCo website – on your phone or any computer. For example: “1½ hours a day, only between 8 am and 7:30 pm”.

2

The PC counts along

A small program on your child's PC counts the minutes. Your child sees how much time is left, and gets a warning 10, 5 and 1 minute before the end.

3

Time's up – screen locks

First the sound goes off, then a lock screen appears. Your child can still save their game and log out. Nothing gets lost.

+

Need more time?

Homework not finished? Tap +15 / +30 / +60 minutes on your phone. Or tell your child an unlock code – that even works without internet.

What your child sees

A friendly lock screen. In your child's language.

When the time is up, this screen covers everything. It can't be closed, moved or tricked with keyboard shortcuts. Your child can type in an unlock code from you – or simply log out.

What you get

Everything on one page. On your phone.

See everything at a glance

For every child: how long they have been on the PC today, how much time is left, whether they are at the PC right now – and which program is open.

  • +15 / +30 / +60 minutes or “unlimited today” – one tap.
  • Lock now – for dinner, homework or bedtime. One child or all.
  • Unlock code – write it on a sticky note or send it by text message. Works even when the PC is offline.

A weekly plan, like a timetable

Choose for each day how many minutes are allowed and between which times. Weekdays different from the weekend? Of course.

  • Copy Monday to all school days with one click.
  • Block single programs (for example a game that isn't age-appropriate).
  • Block websites, switch on SafeSearch and YouTube's restricted mode.
  • Choose what happens at the end: lock screen or log out.
  • Grows with your child: every restriction has its own switch – programs, Windows tools, the Store, private browsing, SafeSearch. Lift them one by one, or turn them back on any time.

Which programs, how long

A 14-day overview shows how much time your child spent on the PC – and which programs: games, browser, homework. No guessing, no arguing. Next to every program there is a button to block it – or to allow it again.

An alert when something is wrong

PaCo watches for dangerous words in searches, window titles and chats – for example about suicide, drugs, violence, bullying, or adults who try to lure children (“don't tell anyone”, “send me a picture”). You can add your own words.

  • You get a push message on your phone right away.
  • Only the hit and a short snippet are sent – not the whole conversation.
  • For suicide-related words, your child also sees where to get help (the helpline of your country).

The PCs – and a safety check

See which PCs are online, which child uses which Windows account – and whether the PC is safe: is there an antivirus, a firewall, a password on the admin account? PaCo checks this every day and tells you what to fix.

  • Screenshot on request: see what is on the screen right now.
  • Where is the laptop? Location on request – helpful if it gets lost or stolen.
  • Updates for the PC program with one click.

Nothing happens in secret

Every attempt to get around PaCo ends up in the log: clock changed, wrong unlock code, blocked program started, PC started in safe mode, PaCo removed.

Hard to trick

Kids are clever. PaCo was built for that.

We tried the usual tricks ourselves – on a real Windows PC.

? “I'll set the clock forward or back.”

Doesn't help. PaCo has its own internal clock and checks it against your server. Changing the Windows or BIOS clock gives no extra time – and you get a message.

? “I'll turn off the Wi-Fi.”

Doesn't help. The rules are stored on the PC. Without internet, PaCo keeps counting and locks just the same.

? “I'll close the program / uninstall it.”

Not possible from a child account. PaCo runs as a protected Windows service. Only an admin can remove it – and then you get a message.

? “I'll use a fake server.”

Doesn't work. Every rule, every extra minute and the time itself are digitally signed by your server. The PC only accepts your server's signature – a fake one is ignored and reported.

? “I'll start Windows in safe mode.”

Doesn't help. PaCo also starts in safe mode and locks the same way – we tested it. You also get a message that the PC was started in safe mode.

? “I'll install another browser or game from the Store.”

Not without you. The Microsoft Store, the command line, PowerShell and the registry editor are locked for your child's account – using the same Windows policies IT departments use. Games and normal programs keep working.

? “I'll use my brother's account.”

You decide which Windows accounts are watched. Every child account gets its own rules; a time budget can count across several PCs.

? “I'll guess the unlock code.”

Not a chance. Codes are only valid for one child and one day, can be used once, and after a few wrong tries input is paused.

Set up

Set up in about 10 minutes

PaCo runs on your own small server (for example a home NAS). One technical person sets that up once – maybe you, maybe a friend. After that, everything below is click-and-done.

For the technical person · once

Start the PaCo server

The server is a single Docker container with a guide: server setup guide. Then invite the parents – each parent gets a personal link by e-mail or chat.

Parent · on your phone

Open your invitation link

Choose a password. Then scan a QR code with an authenticator app (for example Google Authenticator) – this keeps your account safe even if someone knows your password. The website then shows a short 3-step guide.

Parent · on the website

Add your child's PC

Go to Devices → Add your child's PC and click Download installer. Copy it to your child's PC (USB stick, e-mail, or download it there directly).

Parent · on the child's PC

Run the installer – with your admin account

Sign in to the PC with the administrator account (not your child's account) and double-click the installer. Windows asks if it may make changes – click Yes.

Click “Connect”

The server address and the pairing code are already filled in. Just click Connect.

Choose your child's account

PaCo lists all Windows accounts on the PC. Pick which one belongs to which child. Your child doesn't have a Windows account of their own yet? Create one first in Windows (Settings → Accounts → Family & other users) – a normal “standard” account, not an administrator.

Safety check

PaCo checks whether your child could get around it, and whether the PC is protected against viruses. Green is fine. Most other points PaCo fixes by itself. If something red stays – like a missing password on the admin account – please fix it yourself.

Done!

From now on, the rules apply. You manage everything on the website. Tip: set a password for the PC's BIOS too, then nobody can change the clock there.

Parent · on your phone

Get alerts on your phone

Install the free app ntfy (Android or iPhone) and follow the 3 steps on the website under Settings → Phone notifications. Every parent does this once on their own phone.

Languages

Speaks your language – and your child's

Every parent picks their own language on the website. For each child you choose the language of the lock screen. The alert words and the helpline numbers are made for each language and country.

Privacy

Your family's data stays with you

🏠

Your own server

No cloud, no company in between, no ads, no tracking. Everything is stored on your server at home.

🔒

Families are separated

Every family only sees its own children and PCs. Even the server operator doesn't see your children's data.

🧹

Only what is needed

Minutes per program, events and alerts. No chat histories. Screenshots are encrypted and deleted after 7 days.

👀

Open source

Anyone can read the code on GitHub and check what PaCo does. Free to use (MIT licence).

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Does PaCo work on phones, tablets, Macs or Linux?

Not yet. Right now PaCo protects Windows 10 and Windows 11 PCs and laptops. Support for other devices – phones, tablets, Macs, Linux – may very well come in the future. You can already manage PaCo from any phone or computer with a web browser.

Do I need to be good with computers?

For daily use: no – it's tapping buttons on a website. Setting up the server once needs someone who knows a bit about servers or a NAS (about an hour).

What happens when the PC has no internet?

PaCo keeps working with the last rules it received. Time is counted, the screen locks on time, and unlock codes still work. When the PC is back online, everything is sent to the website.

My child really needs the PC for homework. What now?

Give extra time with one tap on your phone, or tell your child an unlock code (for example “30 minutes”). Codes work even without internet and only once.

Does PaCo read my child's messages?

No. PaCo looks for specific dangerous words directly on the PC. Only when a word matches does it send the word, the program and a short snippet around it to you. Nothing else leaves the PC.

Can my child just uninstall it?

Not from a normal child account. PaCo checks that child accounts have no administrator rights. If an admin removes PaCo, you get a push message.

Can both parents use it?

Yes. A parent can invite the other parent (or grandparents) into the family. Everyone uses their own login and their own language.

What does it cost?

Nothing. PaCo is free and open source. You only need a small server, for example a NAS you already have at home.
